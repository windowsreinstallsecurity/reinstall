How we score a mouse

Four categories, weighted evenly, turned into one comparable number out of 10.

01

Sensor accuracy

Tracked against a controlled surface pass to check for smoothing, jitter, and DPI drift at the sensor's rated range.

02

Click & input latency

End-to-end click response measured across polling rates, including wireless mice at both 1000Hz and 8000Hz where supported.

03

Build & materials

Shell flex, switch consistency after repeated clicks, cable or dongle quality, and coating durability under sweat and grip.

04

Value

Performance relative to street price, not MSRP — and whether a cheaper model in the same class gets you 90% of the result.

Terms worth knowing

Short answers, no fluff.

What is polling rate?

How often your mouse reports its position to your PC, measured in Hz. 1000Hz means it reports 1,000 times per second. Higher polling can lower click latency slightly, but the gain shrinks fast past 1000Hz for most players.

Does 8000Hz actually matter?

It reduces the small delay between a physical click and the signal reaching your PC, but the difference versus 1000Hz is a few milliseconds — noticeable at a competitive level, close to unnoticeable for casual play, and it costs battery life on wireless mice.

What grip style should I use?

Palm grip rests your whole hand on the mouse and suits larger, heavier shapes. Claw grip arches the fingers and works well with shorter mice. Fingertip grip only touches with fingertips and favors light, symmetrical shapes. Try each with a mouse you already own before buying based on grip alone.

Does DPI matter more than sensor accuracy?

Not really. Almost every modern sensor tracks accurately well past the DPI most players actually use (800–1600). A cheap sensor at high DPI is still a cheap sensor — accuracy and consistency matter more than the DPI ceiling on the box.
